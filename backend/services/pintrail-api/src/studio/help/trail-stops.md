A trail's **Stops** card lists its stops in order, shows them on a map with numbered pins, and has the controls to change them.

## The walking route

The map draws the walking route through the stops, in order, along real paths and sidewalks, with the total distance and walking time underneath. Each stop also says how far it is from the one before, such as *520 m · 6 min walk from stop 3*.

- **When it's worked out:** automatically, a few seconds after you add, remove or reorder stops, or after a stop's location changes. While that happens the card says *Working out the walking route…* and updates itself.
- **Same building:** stops in the same building are the same spot, so the walk between them shows as *Same spot as the last stop*.
- **No route:** if no route can be found, for example because a stop is far from any mapped path, the card says why and the map joins the stops with a dashed straight line instead.
- **What it's based on:** the route comes from OpenStreetMap, so it only knows the paths that are mapped there. Your **Getting here** directions are what walkers read; the route line is there to show the way on the map.

## Adding a stop

1. Under **Add a stop**, choose an artifact. The list shows every artifact, with nested ones under their building, like *Integrative Learning Center › Green roof*.
2. Optionally, add **Getting here**: how to walk to this stop from the last one.
3. Optionally, add a **note**: what to look for once you're there.
4. Click **+ Add stop**. It goes on the end.

Any artifact can be a stop, whoever added it. [Topics](/studio/help/topics) can't, since they aren't places.

> **Example:** Stop 1: *Integrative Learning Center › Green roof*, note *Look up: the roof is planted with sedum.* Stop 2: *Stormwater reuse for irrigation*, getting here *Walk around to the north side of the building*, note *The planted beds along the path take the runoff.*

## Putting stops in order

Drag a stop by its **grip** (the six dots on its left) to a new place in the list. Or click the grip and use the <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> keys. The numbers and the map update to match, so you can check the route makes sense.

## Getting here

Directions take someone from one stop to the next on foot. Many campus paths have no name, so write what a person can see: landmarks, which side things are on, roughly how far.

- **Good:** *With the pond on your left, head for the brick chimney. The plant is the building right below it, about five minutes.*
- **Not useful:** *Go north on the footpath.*
- On **stop 1** it's labelled *Getting to the start*: say where the walk begins and how to find it, such as which parking lot or bus stop.
- The app shows a stop's directions before the stop, so walkers read them on the way.

## Notes

A good note is short and practical: **what to look for** once you're there. The artifact's own description is shown too, so don't repeat it.

- Click the **pencil** to change a stop's directions or note.
- Up to 1,000 characters, but a sentence or two is better.

## Removing a stop

Click the **×** on the stop. The artifact itself isn't affected.

## Stops that aren't ready

If a stop's artifact isn't approved yet, the card says so. Explorers see that stop as **not available** until it's approved. Get every stop approved before asking for the trail to be made public.

A stop whose artifact has no location yet isn't on the map; the list says so.

> **Note:** If a stop's artifact is deleted, the stop stays on the trail marked *deleted*, so you know to replace it.

Up to 200 stops per trail.
