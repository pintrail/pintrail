Someone will read your description **standing in front of the artifact, on their phone**. Write for them: a visitor who's curious but has never heard of it.

## What to say

A good description answers three questions, usually in this order:

1. **What is it?** One or two sentences anyone would understand.
2. **Why does it matter?** The sustainability story, with **a real number** where you have one: megawatts, gallons, percent saved, the year it opened.
3. **How does it work, or what should I notice?** Something to look at or think about while standing there.

Put your **sources** in the [Links](/studio/help/links) card, not in the text.

## An example

> **Example:** *A 1-megawatt / 4-megawatt-hour lithium-ion battery at the Central Heating Plant, funded by a $1.1 million state grant in December 2017.*
>
> *Why it matters: the battery stores electricity when demand is low and releases it at peak times. That lowers the campus's peak demand, makes it easier to use renewable power such as solar, and helps keep the grid steady.*
>
> *The plant isn't open to visitors, so this is one to read about from outside: the battery is the plant's way of saving power for when campus needs it most.*

Compare a weak one: *"This is a battery. It stores energy. It is good for the environment."* It's true, but it says nothing a visitor couldn't guess, and has no numbers.

## An example for search and questions

Illustrative only; replace this with verified information about the actual place.

> **Example:** This rain garden collects runoff from the surrounding paved area.
> Water gathers temporarily and gradually soaks into the soil, helping reduce
> surface runoff. [Add verified details about this particular garden and location.]

Add its actual sources in **Links**. The description could answer “How does this
garden reduce runoff?” or help a search for “stormwater management.” It cannot
answer a construction-cost question without additional evidence.

## How to write it

**Do not invent facts.** Omit details you cannot verify or ask Tim or the campus
sustainability office. Example records illustrate a writing style; verify the
facts before using them for a real artifact.

- **Short paragraphs.** Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> twice between them. The Studio keeps your line breaks. Three or four short paragraphs is plenty.
- **Your own words.** Don't paste text from a website. Summarize it, and link the page as a source.
- **Only what you can source.** If you're not sure of a number, leave it out, or ask the campus sustainability office.
- **Plain language.** Explain a term the first time (*combined heat and power: making electricity and heat in the same place*), or link a [topic](/studio/help/topics) that explains it.
- **Present tense, no "I".** *The roof holds rainwater*, not *I think the roof holds rainwater*.

> **Note:** If you know Markdown, you can use it for **bold**, *italics*, and lists. The Studio shows the text as you typed it for now; the app is planned to format it.

## For a topic

A topic's description is shown on **every** linked artifact, so write only what's true of all of them. Anything that differs from one artifact to the next (a building's LEED level, a station's number of docks) goes on that artifact or in the note on its link. See [Creating a topic](/studio/help/creating-a-topic).
